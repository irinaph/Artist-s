# Artist's Way Practitioners: Where They Stall, and Do They Want Patterns?
**Phase 2: Discovery · desk research · 2026-11-16**
**Questions** (from `my-past-attempts.md`):
1. Do other people also stall in weeks 1–2, and is it for emotional or permission reasons, like me?
2. Do practitioners want to see patterns across their pages, or does that break the practice?
3. How do they track the course today?

**Method:** web search, then reading 12 first-person accounts (Substack, Medium, newsletters), 4 Notion/printable trackers, and secondary summaries of expressive-writing research.

## How strong is the evidence?
| Tier | What it is | Trust |
|---|---|---|

## Q1. Do others stall early, and why?

### Finding 1: Yes, early, but the cliff runs through weeks 2–5, not only days 1–14
- One newsletter poll found that **74% had started the book but never finished it** (self-selected, n not stated). [B]
- Where accounts name a stall point, it falls between **week 1 and week 5**:
  - pages for 3 days, then the interest faded within a week or two (Gulotta)
  - repeatedly failed after "a week or two at a time" and gave the book away (Lam)
  - "a total fail at week 3", and considered quitting (Mulligan)
  - earlier attempts ended "around week 3" (Gulotta); "by week 4 or 5" it feels like a second job (Internet People)
- Several writers describe a **snowball**: a few missed pages lead to a skipped artist date, and then the book gathers dust. [B]

> **What it means for us:** my own stalls (day 3 and day ~10) are at the early end of a common pattern. The danger zone looks closer to **weeks 1–4** than days 1–14.

### Finding 2: Their reasons match mine (permission, time, emotional weight), plus two I didn't have
| Reason | Me | Others |
|---|---|---|
| **Permission / guilt** | "Too old, too many responsibilities" | Mothers feel intense guilt over "non-productive" time; one writer calls it shame rather than guilt. A midlife writer calls a weekly date nearly laughable next to kids, work and caregiving. Spending money on yourself is hard [B] |
| **Time** | The artist date lost to responsibilities | The **artist date is the most-named sticking point**: hard to fit in, and it "feels like goofing off" [B] |
| **Emotional weight** | Anger I couldn't regroup from | Pages can surface suppressed feelings and cause a few weeks of disruption; one reviewer advises against starting in a turbulent period [B]. Research: expressive writing **raises distress and negative mood in the short term**, with benefits later (stronger for physical than emotional health) [A] |
| **Rigidity / homework** *(new)* | — | Too many rules and tasks; 12 weeks feels daunting; "one more thing on my plate" [B] |
| **All-or-nothing** *(new)* | — | Perfectionism; missing a day feels like failing the course (same mechanism as broken streaks in `user-complaints.md`) [B] |
| **Pressure on the artist date** *(new nuance)* | — | Unsure what counts; feels the date must be inspiring, and a dull one feels wasted [B] |

> **What it means for us:** the days 1–14 support (permission framing, small dates count, a calm next step on heavy days) **addresses real, shared reasons**, not just mine. Rigidity and all-or-nothing thinking back our existing decisions (no streak resets, weeks never lock, pause or continue).

### Finding 3: What got people through was lowering the bar
- **Shrinking the practice:** 1–1.5 pages instead of 3; notes in the margin; a "bare minimum" version with a zero-shame rule. [B]
- **Own pace:** a month per chapter; letting a hard week take longer. [B]
- **Small, calendared dates:** a library or café counts; put the date in the calendar like any appointment. [B]
- **Company:** a group or a public weekly check-in. One facilitator calls doing it in a group game-changing. [B]

> **What it means for us:** this supports "a small date counts" and the "pause or continue" choice. It also raises a question we haven't decided: **should a lighter morning (fewer than 3 pages) count as "Pages done"?** (candidate below)

## Q2. Do practitioners want patterns, or does it break the practice?

### Finding 4: The book's guidance is about *rereading*, and its reason is self-censorship
- Secondary sources agree the book advises **not rereading your pages for roughly the first eight weeks**. The reason: knowing you'll read it back makes you write for an audience, and the inner critic creeps in. (Paraphrased; no book text used.)
- **Our capture is not rereading.** It happens *after* writing, in my own words, and the app never sees the pages. **The risk is indirect:** if I know I'll tag a mood, I might start writing *for the tag*.

### Finding 5: A real segment does want patterns, and they get them by separating writing from reviewing
- **Yearly review:** one writer read a year of pages (~400) and sorted them into recurring themes. She found she loves cooking more than she knew and saw where she'd grown or was still stuck. [B]
- **Rereading at a low point:** another reread her early pages during a flat stretch, saw progress on goals she'd forgotten, and it pulled her back from quitting. She openly disagrees with the no-reread rule. [B]
- **A full review system:** a Forte Labs writer scans, transcribes, highlights and tags his pages. He argues the no-review rule leaves insight on the table. [B]
- **Tags and summaries:** a Notion template adds a one-line summary field and tags for recurring themes and emotions. [C]
- **Auto-analysis:** the *Morning Pages* app runs NLP emotion stats on *typed* pages, with weekly and monthly views. It's a vendor claim with no user evidence, and it requires typing. [C]
- **Not everyone:** some practitioners never review, and a critic of the practice found her insights elsewhere. [B]

> **What it means for us:** wanting patterns isn't just me. Every way practitioners get them today is **heavy** (rereading 400 pages, scanning and transcribing, a Notion database) or **gives up paper** (typing into an app). **A 10-second capture in my own words, after writing, is a lighter version of what they already do by hand.** That's the clearest differentiator so far.
> **The design rule they've found:** keep writing and reviewing apart. The capture comes after the pages, and the pattern view is something I go to, not something that interrupts the writing.

## Q3. How do they track the course today?
| Method | What it tracks | What's missing |
|---|---|---|
| **A dated notebook** (me, most people) | Where I am | No overview |
| **Notion templates** (4 found: free to ~$15) | A checkbox per day for pages, artist date, weekly check-in pages, progress | Checkboxes only; no patterns; setup effort; desktop-first |
| **Printable PDF trackers** (~$5) | Pages, artist dates, walks | Paper-only, no overview |
| **Public weekly check-ins** on Substack (very common) | Weekly reflections, for accountability | Public; no private patterns |
| **Morning Pages app** | Typed pages + emotion stats | Not paper-first; streaks *(verify in competitors.md)* |

> **What it means for us:** the "trackers" are all **checkbox logs**; none turns the practice into a picture over time. The many public weekly check-ins also show that **accountability through sharing is real**, which supports the v1.1 share card (`ideas.md`), not the MVP.

## What this changes in our concept
| Concept element | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Brief v2: the app reflects my patterns back | ✅ Supported | A real segment wants this; current methods are heavy or require typing |
| Paper-first; the app never sees the pages | ✅ Strongly supported | Respects the book's privacy and self-censorship concern |
| Capture after the tap, optional | ✅ Supported | Matches how practitioners keep writing and reviewing apart |
| Extra support in days 1–14 | ⚠️ Sharpened | Window stretched to weeks 1–4, tapering (accepted 2026-11-21) |
| A small artist date counts | ✅ Strongly supported | The artist date is the most-named sticking point |
| Heavy-day prompt (calm next step) | ✅ Supported | Short-term distress is expected (research); keep it calm, never therapy |
| No streak resets; pause or continue | ✅ Supported | All-or-nothing thinking and the snowball are named quit reasons |
| Share card in v1.1 | ➕ Evidence for it | Public weekly check-ins are a common way people stay accountable |

## Candidates → all four accepted 2026-11-21 (see Decisions in `project-plan.md`)
1. **Stretch the early-support window** from days 1–14 to **weeks 1–4**, tapering off. Small change: same features, longer window. **My recommendation: yes.** Trade-off: support that stays too long can feel patronizing, so taper it.
2. **A "light pages" option:** "Pages done" also counts a shorter morning (e.g. one page). Small change. **My recommendation: yes, as a quiet option** behind the tap, never the default ask. Trade-off: it bends the book's three-page rule, so the wording must stay neutral.
3. **Pattern view timing:** visible from day 1, or **quiet until a few weeks of captures exist** (echoing the book's wait-before-rereading). **My recommendation:** visible but low-key in weeks 1–2 (a "your first patterns appear after about a week" empty state), with no insights pushed at me. This keeps the success criterion "one pattern I didn't notice after 2 weeks."
4. **Heavy-day copy should say a dip is normal early on.** It's our own words, backed by the expressive-writing research. A copy decision for Phase 4.
